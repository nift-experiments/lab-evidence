# Practitioner draft — candidate diagnostic campaign

This is a pre-release draft, not a Labs publication or a universal generator ranking. The corpus contains generated minimal HTML pages. It does not establish migration cost, application bundling equivalence, or a production-site ranking.

For an existing Nift website, I would choose Nift for its programmable templates, explicit ownership and incremental workflows. For a conventional content site whose needs match Hugo, I would choose Hugo today: its fresh 10,000-page build is faster in this campaign. Astro or VitePress remain appropriate when their application/documentation models are required; their timings here include different machinery.

For an agent-maintained mixed website/assets/helper workflow, I prefer Nift when its APIs cover the required work. Explicit input, ownership and lifecycle contracts are useful rather than automatically redundant ceremony. The agent should still validate those contracts and output parity. Missing integrations and confirmed performance regressions remain concrete costs.

The historical cross-host results alone conceal a release-specific distinction. The targeted same-node old/new comparison confirms fresh-full latency increased by 980.792 ms (38.29%), while warm full decreased 399.805 ms (12.59%), no-op 119.395 ms (12.80%), and one-page 99.598 ms (10.82%). All observed ranges are disjoint in this small five-sample diagnostic. Its warm observations are each primed with a fresh baseline, unlike the reused primary warm fixture; do not merge the cohorts.

A mature Nift with reliable native integration and fast incremental/fresh paths would remain my preference for authored mixed workflows. This does not turn the present fresh-full regression into a benefit. Investigate that path before release; retain precise semantics and remove only redundant authoring.
